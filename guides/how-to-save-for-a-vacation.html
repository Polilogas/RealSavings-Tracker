TRAVEL SAVINGS

How to Save Money for a Vacation

Everybody likes to go on vacation. But going on vacation needs a proper plan, and it needs money.

START WITH WHY

Know what you are saving for.

Saving money for a vacation can be difficult when you are only thinking about the money you are putting away.

Every time you decide not to spend that money on something else, you need to remember why you are saving it in the first place.

Maybe you want to explore a new country. Maybe you want to spend time with your wife, your husband, your friends, or your family. Maybe you simply want to take a break from everyday life.

Remember why you started

When you lose motivation, think about the experience you are saving for. The money is not the final goal. The vacation is.

MAKE IT SPECIFIC

"Vacation" is not really a goal.

One of the first things you should do is make your savings goal specific.

Instead of creating a goal called "Vacation", give it a clear purpose. For example:

Visit Amsterdam with my wife
Japan trip 2027
Family holiday in Spain

A specific goal makes it easier to remember what you are working toward. It also gives you a much clearer idea of how much money you actually need.

KNOW YOUR TARGET

Figure out how much you need.

Once you know what you are saving for, estimate how much the vacation will cost.

Think about the main expenses involved in the trip. Depending on your vacation, this could include:

Flights
Accommodation
Food
Activities
Transportation
Extra spending

You do not need to know the exact final price. An honest estimate is enough to start building your plan.

CHOOSE YOUR END DATE CAREFULLY

Give yourself enough time.

Choosing an end date is one of the most important parts of creating a savings goal.

It can be tempting to choose a short deadline because you want your vacation to happen as soon as possible. But if the amount you need to save every month is more than you can realistically afford, the plan will probably fail.

A longer goal can be a better goal

It is better to choose a realistic end date that you can actually reach than a short date that puts too much pressure on your budget.

If you can comfortably save €100 per month, but your plan requires €250 per month, consider giving yourself more time. Your vacation may happen later, but your chances of actually reaching the goal become much higher.

REAL LIFE EXAMPLES

Two people, two very different plans.

Let's look at two simple examples. Both people want to go on vacation, but they approach their savings goal differently.

EXAMPLE 1

Maria made a realistic plan.

Maria wants to visit Amsterdam with her wife. She estimates that the trip will cost €1,500.

Target €1,500
Monthly savings €100
Time needed 15 months

Maria could have chosen a much shorter deadline, but €100 per month fits comfortably into her budget. She knows exactly what she wants, how much she needs, and when she can realistically reach it.

EXAMPLE 2

Alex chose a deadline he could not afford.

Alex also wants a €1,500 vacation. He wants to go in six months, which means he needs to save €250 every month.

Target €1,500
Monthly savings €250
Time available 6 months

The problem is that Alex cannot realistically save €250 every month after paying for his normal expenses. After a few months, he starts falling behind and eventually gives up on the goal.

Alex would have been better off choosing a longer timeline and saving an amount that actually fits his budget.

MAKE THE CALCULATION

Turn your target into a monthly plan.

Once you know your target and your end date, you can work out approximately how much you need to save each month.

MONTHLY SAVINGS €1,500 ÷ 15 months = €100

In this example, saving €100 every month for 15 months would give you the €1,500 needed for the vacation.

If the monthly amount feels too high, do not simply force yourself to save more. Go back to your goal and adjust the end date or the amount you plan to spend.

STAY MOTIVATED

What if you lose motivation?

This is probably one of the hardest parts of saving money. At the beginning, seeing your savings grow can feel exciting. After a few months, however, putting money away can start to feel like a sacrifice.

When that happens, remember why you created the goal.

What am I saving this money for? ?

Think about the moment you will finally take the trip. Think about the places you will see, the people you will spend time with, and the experiences you are working toward.

The goal is not to make saving money feel exciting every single month. The goal is to keep remembering why the goal matters to you.

TRACK YOUR PROGRESS

Watch yourself get closer.

Saving €100 can feel small when your goal is €1,500. But when you track your progress, you can see that the money is actually moving you closer to the trip.

AMSTERDAM TRIP €900 / €1,500
60%

You have already saved 60% of your target.

Seeing progress like this can make the goal feel much more real. You are no longer just trying to save money. You are building toward something specific.

YOUR PLAN CAN CHANGE

Adjust the goal when life changes.

Your original plan might not always work perfectly. Flights can become more expensive, your income can change, or you might decide that you would rather wait a little longer.

That does not mean you failed. It means your plan needs to change.

ORIGINAL PLAN €150 / month Vacation in 10 months
NEW PLAN €100 / month Vacation in 15 months

A slower plan can still get you where you want to go. The important thing is that the new plan remains realistic.

EVERYTHING COUNTS

Small savings still move you forward.

You do not always need to make a large deposit. Sometimes you might only be able to put €20 or €30 toward your vacation goal.

That is still progress.

SMALL DEPOSIT €20 today
→
VACATION FUND €20+ closer to your trip

The important thing is to keep the goal moving in the right direction without putting yourself in a difficult financial situation.
