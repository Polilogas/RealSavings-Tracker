TRAVEL SAVINGS

How to Save Money for a Vacation

You probably want to go on vacation, but you also know that vacations cost money. So how can you actually save enough to go without spending money you do not have? Well, let's look at how you can make a plan that works for you.

START WITH WHY

Know what you are saving for.

Before you start saving, think about why you want to go on vacation. Are you tired of your everyday routine and want to take a break? Do you want to visit another country or spend some time with the people you love?

The reason I am telling you this is because saving money is not always easy. There will probably be times when you want to buy something else, and you will have to decide whether to spend that money or put it toward your vacation.

For example, maybe you want to visit Amsterdam with your wife, travel to Japan, or take your family somewhere nice. When you know exactly what you are saving for, it becomes easier to remember why you started in the first place.

Remember why you started

You are not saving money just to watch a number grow. You are saving because there is something you want to do with that money.

MAKE IT SPECIFIC

"Vacation" is not really a goal.

Now, let's say you open RealSavings Tracker and create a savings goal called "Vacation". There is nothing wrong with that, but what does it actually mean? Where do you want to go? How much money do you need?

Instead, give your goal a clear purpose. For example:

Visit Amsterdam with my wife
Japan trip 2027
Family holiday in Spain

See the difference? Now you know what you are working toward. You can start looking at prices, estimate how much the trip will cost, and decide how much you need to save.

The more specific your goal is, the easier it becomes to turn it into a plan.

KNOW YOUR TARGET

Figure out how much you need.

Once you know where you want to go, the next thing you need to do is estimate how much the vacation will cost. You do not need to know the exact price of everything, but you should have a general idea before you start saving.

Think about all the things you will need to pay for. Depending on your trip, this could include:

Flights
Accommodation
Food
Activities
Transportation
Extra spending

For example, if you think your trip will cost around €1,500, you can use that as your initial target. Just remember that it is an estimate. Prices can change, and you might end up spending more than you expected.

It is better to have a realistic estimate and adjust it later than to start saving without knowing how much money you actually need.

CHOOSE YOUR END DATE CAREFULLY

Give yourself enough time.

Now comes another important part: deciding when you want to reach your goal. Maybe you want to go on vacation in six months, or maybe you are planning a trip for next year.

Of course, everyone wants to go on vacation as soon as possible. But think about it. If you need €1,500 and want to save it in six months, that means you need to put aside €250 every month.

Can you actually afford to save €250 every month after paying for your normal expenses? If the answer is no, then you need to change your plan. Otherwise, you might start saving, realize that you cannot keep up, and give up on the goal completely.

A longer goal can be a better goal

You do not have to rush. Choose an end date that gives you enough time to save without putting pressure on your normal expenses.

If you can comfortably save €100 per month, you could choose a longer timeline instead. Yes, you will have to wait longer for your vacation, but at least your plan will be something you can realistically follow.

REAL LIFE EXAMPLES

Two people, two very different plans.

Let's look at two people who both want to go on a €1,500 vacation. The difference is how they plan to save for it.

EXAMPLE 1

Maria made a realistic plan.

Maria wants to visit Amsterdam with her wife. She estimates that the trip will cost €1,500. She knows she can put aside €100 every month without struggling to pay her other expenses.

Target €1,500
Monthly savings €100
Time needed 15 months

Maria understands that she will need 15 months to reach her target if she saves €100 every month and starts with no money saved. It might seem like a long time, but she has a plan she can actually follow.

EXAMPLE 2

Alex chose a deadline he could not afford.

Alex also wants to spend €1,500 on a vacation. But he wants to go in six months, which means he needs to save €250 every month.

Target €1,500
Monthly savings €250
Time available 6 months

The problem is that Alex cannot afford to save €250 every month after paying his other expenses. He tries anyway, but after a while he starts falling behind and eventually gives up on the goal.

What should Alex have done? He could have chosen a later vacation date or looked for ways to reduce the cost of the trip. There is no point in making a plan that does not fit your financial situation.

MAKE THE CALCULATION

Turn your target into a monthly plan.

Once you know how much your vacation will cost and when you want to go, you can calculate how much you need to save each month.

MONTHLY SAVINGS €1,500 ÷ 15 months = €100

In this example, you need to save €100 every month for 15 months to reach €1,500, assuming you start with no savings and the total cost stays the same.

What if €100 per month is too much for you? Well, you have two choices. You can give yourself more time, or you can look at the trip again and see whether you can reduce the cost.

You do not need to force yourself to save money that you need for food, bills, or other important expenses. Your plan should work with your budget, not against it.

STAY MOTIVATED

What if you lose motivation?

Let's be honest. Saving money for a vacation can feel exciting at first, especially when you have just created your goal. But after a few months, you might start wondering why it is taking so long.

You keep putting money aside, but you still cannot book the trip. Meanwhile, you might see other things you want to buy, and spending that money can feel much more tempting than saving it.

What am I saving this money for? ?

When that happens, remind yourself why you started. Think about the country you want to visit, the places you want to see, and the experience you are saving for. You are working toward something you want to do.

Of course, you do not have to feel motivated every single day. Sometimes you just need to keep following your plan, as long as it still makes sense for your situation.

TRACK YOUR PROGRESS

See how much closer you are.

Imagine that your vacation costs €1,500 and you have already saved €900. If you only look at the amount you still need, you might feel like you have a long way to go.

But you have already saved more than half of your target. That is worth remembering.

AMSTERDAM TRIP €900 / €1,500
60%

You have already saved 60% of your target.

This is why tracking your progress can help. Instead of just putting money away and wondering how much you have left, you can see how far you have come.

Every time you add money, you get closer to your vacation. Even if you cannot save a large amount every month, your progress still counts.

YOUR PLAN CAN CHANGE

Adjust the goal when life changes.

Now, what happens if your original plan stops working? Maybe your expenses increase, your income changes, or you find out that flights and accommodation cost more than you expected.

You do not have to abandon your vacation just because something changed. You can adjust your goal instead.

ORIGINAL PLAN €150 / month Vacation in 10 months
NEW PLAN €100 / month Vacation in 15 months

For example, maybe you planned to save €150 every month, but you realize that €100 is a more realistic amount. You can lower your monthly target and give yourself more time to reach your goal.

Your vacation might happen later than you originally planned, but that is better than constantly struggling to follow a plan you cannot afford.

EVERYTHING COUNTS

Small savings still move you forward.

You do not have to wait until you have a large amount of money available before adding to your vacation fund. Maybe this month you can save €100, but next month you can only put aside €20.

That is still €20 closer to your goal.

SMALL DEPOSIT €20 today
→
VACATION FUND €20+ closer to your trip

Think about it. If you do not save that €20, you will still have the same amount saved as before. But if you put it toward your vacation, you have made some progress, even if it is a small amount.

Just make sure you are not putting yourself in a difficult situation by saving money you need for essential expenses. Save what you can, and keep going when your situation allows it.
