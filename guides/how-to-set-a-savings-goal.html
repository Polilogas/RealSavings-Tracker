SAVINGS GOALS

How to Set a Savings Goal You Can Actually Reach

You probably thought many times that you should save more money. Maybe you even tried, but somehow you still cannot save enough. Well, let's look at how you can give your savings a purpose and make a plan you can actually follow.

Saying "I should save more money" is a good start, but it does not really tell you what you are going to do. How much do you want to save? What are you saving for? And when do you want to reach your goal?

Think about something you actually want or need. Maybe it is a new phone, a vacation, a better computer, a car, some new furniture, or even money for an unexpected expense. Instead of just trying to save money without a clear reason, give it a purpose.

Once you know what you are saving for, you can estimate how much you need and decide how much you can put aside every month.

Remember

You do not always need to reach 100% of your target to make good use of the money you saved.

STEP 1

Give your savings a purpose

The first thing you need to do is decide what you are actually saving for.

For example, you could create a goal called "Savings", but that does not tell you much. Instead, give it a specific purpose.

Here are a few examples:

New phone
Vacation
Emergency fund
New computer
Dental treatment
New furniture

Now you have a reason to save. Every time you add money to that goal, you know what you are working toward. And if you suddenly want to spend that money on something else, you can think about whether it is really worth changing your plan.

STEP 2

Decide how much you need

Now that you know what you are saving for, the next step is to estimate how much money you will need.

You do not always need an exact number. Maybe the price changes, you find a better deal, or you decide that another option makes more sense for you.

REAL-LIFE EXAMPLE

Maria wants a new phone

Maria finds a phone she likes that costs €900. She already has €200 saved. Does that mean she needs to save another €900? No. She already has €200, so she only needs another €700.

Phone €900
Already saved €200
Still needed €700

This is why it helps to keep track of what you have already saved. You do not have to start from zero every time you create a goal. The money you already have counts too.

STEP 3

Think about when you would like to reach it

Now comes the question: when do you want to reach your goal?

Let's go back to Maria. She wants to buy the €900 phone, already has €200 saved, and would like to buy it in seven months.

AMOUNT REMAINING €900 - €200 = €700

She needs another €700. If she wants to save that amount over seven months, the calculation is pretty simple.

MONTHLY SAVINGS €700 ÷ 7 = €100 per month

So instead of just saying "I need €900 for a phone", Maria now knows that she needs to save around €100 every month to reach her target in seven months, assuming she starts with the €200 she already has.

STEP 4

Make the plan realistic

Now, this is where you need to think about your actual financial situation.

Maybe Maria looks at her budget and realizes that saving €100 every month is too difficult. She has bills, food, transportation, and other things she needs to pay for. She cannot just ignore those expenses because she wants a phone.

What can she do? She could keep the €900 target but give herself more time to reach it. For example, she could save €70 every month instead and buy the phone later.

There is nothing wrong with taking longer.

It is better to have a plan you can follow than one that looks good on paper but does not work in real life.

The same applies to you. If your monthly target is too high, do not force yourself to save money you need for essential expenses. Adjust the amount or give yourself more time.

STEP 5

You don't always have to reach 100%

This is something I really want you to understand about savings goals. Reaching 100% is great, of course, but not reaching your original target does not mean that the money you saved was wasted.

Let's go back to Maria and her €900 phone.

Imagine that she manages to save €700, but then decides she does not want to wait any longer to buy the phone.

Did she fail? No.

Think about it. Before she started saving, she had €200. Now she has €900 in total if she adds her original €200 to the €700 she saved toward the goal. She is much closer to buying the phone than she was before.

If Maria has saved €700 toward the phone, she has a few options. She can wait and save the remaining €200, look for the same phone at a lower price, or decide to buy a different phone that costs less.

She could also consider another way of paying for the difference, but she should think carefully about the total cost and whether she can afford the payments before doing so.

NEW PHONE €700 / €900
78%

Maria has already saved around 78% of her target.

The point is that she has managed to save €700 toward something she wanted. That is real progress. Her goal helped her put money aside instead of spending it on other things.

Of course, if the phone is something she really wants and she can afford to wait, she can keep saving until she reaches the full amount. But she should not think that everything was pointless just because she did not reach 100%.

STEP 6

Real life can change your goals

Sometimes you start saving for something, and then something else becomes more important. Maybe you need money for a necessary expense, or you simply change your mind about what you want to buy.

Well, that is normal. Your priorities can change, and your savings plan can change with them.

REAL-LIFE EXAMPLE

Alex has two savings goals

NEW HARRY POTTER WAND €50 saved
DENTAL TREATMENT €0 saved

Alex has saved €50 toward a new Harry Potter wand, but he now realizes that he needs to pay for dental treatment. He decides that the dental treatment is more important and moves the €50 toward that goal.

The wand goal now has €0 saved, but that does not mean Alex has lost the progress he made. He has simply decided to use his money for something more important right now.

He can always start saving for the wand again later. The important thing is to make a decision that makes sense for his situation.

STEP 7

Be careful when moving money between goals

There is one thing you should remember if you have several savings goals. Moving money from one goal to another does not create any new money. It just changes which goal that money is being saved for.

For example, imagine you have €400 saved for a new phone and €50 saved for a dental visit.

NEW PHONE €400 before
→
DENTAL VISIT €50 before

If you move the €50 from the dental goal to the phone goal, you will have €450 saved for the phone, but nothing left in the dental goal. Your total savings have not increased. You have just moved the money around.

Don't move money just to finish a goal.

Move it because you have decided that another goal is more important right now.

If something important comes up, such as dental treatment or replacing a broken device you need, it can make sense to change your priorities.

But if you keep moving money around just to make one goal look completed, you might lose track of what you actually have saved for each thing. Make sure you know where your money is going and why.

STEP 8

Track what actually happens

Once you have created your savings goals, start recording the money you actually put aside. Do not just rely on what you planned to save. Keep track of what really happens.

Maybe you planned to save €100 this month but only managed to save €70. That is okay. At least you know what you managed to put aside.

Maybe next month you can save €150 instead. That is good too. You do not have to save exactly the same amount every month.

Over time, you will start to see how much you actually save and how long it takes you to reach your goals.

VACATION €500 / €2,000
25%

You have already saved 25% of your target.

This is where a savings tracker can help. Instead of trying to remember everything in your head, you can see how much you have saved, how much remains, and which goals need your attention.

REAL-LIFE EXAMPLE

Saving for a vacation

Let's put everything together with one final example. Alex wants to go on vacation and estimates that the trip will cost around €2,000.

He already has €500 saved and wants to travel in about ten months.

Target €2,000
Already saved €500
Remaining €1,500
Time available 10 months
Monthly target €150

Alex needs another €1,500. If he saves €150 every month for ten months, he will reach €2,000 in total, assuming the vacation still costs the same amount.

But what if he reaches €1,700 and finds a cheaper vacation than he expected? He might not need the full €2,000 anymore.

Or maybe the trip ends up costing more than expected. In that case, he can decide whether to keep saving for longer, change his plans, or look for ways to reduce the cost.

His savings goal helped him prepare, but it did not have to predict everything perfectly. That is the whole point of having a plan: you have a direction to follow, and you can adjust it when you need to.

A savings goal is a guide, not a rule.

02

Choose a target that makes sense for you.

03

Save what you can without neglecting essential expenses.

04

05

Change your goals when your priorities change.

06

Keep track of what you have actually saved.

Your savings are there to help you

The whole idea of creating a savings goal is to help you manage your money and work toward something that matters to you.

Give your money a purpose, choose a target, and think about how much you can realistically save. If you have a date in mind, work out how much you need to put aside each month.

But do not think that you have failed just because you do not reach the exact target. Maybe you changed your mind, maybe prices changed, or maybe another expense became more important.

And remember what we discussed earlier. If you wanted to save €900 for a phone but managed to save €700, you are still €700 closer to buying it than you were before you started.

You can keep saving, change the goal, or decide to use that money for something more important. The decision is yours.

Just make sure you know what you are saving for and keep track of where your money goes. That way, you can make decisions based on what you actually have, instead of guessing.
