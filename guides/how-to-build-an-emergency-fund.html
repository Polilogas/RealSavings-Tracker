EMERGENCY FUND

How to Build an Emergency Fund

An unexpected bill, an urgent repair, or a sudden change in your income can put pressure on your finances. An emergency fund gives you money set aside specifically for these situations, helping you handle unexpected expenses without relying entirely on borrowing.

BE PREPARED

Why do you need an emergency fund?

Most household expenses are predictable. You know that you will need money for food, housing, transportation, bills, and other everyday necessities. These costs can usually be included in your regular budget.

Unexpected expenses are different. A household appliance might stop working, your car might need an urgent repair, or you might have an unexpected medical expense. You may also experience a period when your income is lower than usual.

Without savings, even a relatively small expense can make it harder to pay your other bills. You might need to borrow money, delay another payment, or use money that was intended for a different purpose.

An emergency fund cannot prevent unexpected events, and it may not cover every possible expense. However, having some money available can give you more options when something goes wrong.

The purpose is financial flexibility

Emergency savings give you money to turn to when an important expense appears unexpectedly. Even a modest reserve can be a useful starting point.

CHOOSE YOUR FIRST TARGET

How much should you save for emergencies?

There is no single emergency fund target that works for everyone. The amount you need depends on your essential expenses, income stability, household responsibilities, and the other financial support available to you.

If you are starting with no savings, thinking about a large target can feel overwhelming. Instead, choose an initial milestone that seems achievable. Your first target could be €100, €300, or another amount that makes sense for your circumstances.

For example, suppose you choose an initial target of €300 and can save €25 each month. If you make each planned contribution and do not withdraw any money, you would reach that target in 12 months.

Once you reach your first milestone, you can decide whether to increase your target. One way to plan for a larger emergency fund is to calculate your essential monthly expenses and consider how long you might need to cover them if your income were interrupted.

For instance, if your essential expenses total €800 a month, a reserve of €800 would represent approximately one month of those expenses. A longer-term target could cover several months, depending on your needs and what you can reasonably save.

Treat these amounts as planning examples, not requirements. Your first savings milestone is useful even if you are still a long way from your eventual target.

START WITH WHAT YOU CAN

Small contributions can add up.

You might feel that there is not enough money left at the end of the month to build an emergency fund. Before choosing a contribution, consider your essential expenses and any payments you need to make.

If you can comfortably put aside €10, €20, or €30 a month, you can start with that amount. You do not need to wait until you can save hundreds of euros at a time.

If you cannot afford to save right now without missing essential payments, it is reasonable to focus on those needs first. Your contribution can change when your circumstances change.

KEEP IT ACCESSIBLE

Where should you keep your emergency savings?

Where you keep your emergency fund matters because you may need to access the money quickly. An account that makes your savings difficult to withdraw could be less suitable for unexpected expenses.

A savings account with a regulated bank may be an option worth considering. Before choosing an account, check whether you can withdraw money when needed, whether fees apply, whether there are withdrawal restrictions, and whether your deposits qualify for applicable deposit protection.

You may also find it helpful to keep your emergency savings separate from the account you use for everyday spending. This makes the amount reserved for emergencies easier to identify and track.

Prioritize access and reliability

Money intended for emergencies should generally be kept somewhere you can access when needed. Investments can lose value and may not be suitable for money you might need at short notice.

KNOW THE PURPOSE

What should your emergency fund cover?

An emergency fund is intended for important expenses that are unexpected and difficult to handle using your regular income alone. It is not possible to list every situation in advance, so it helps to consider both urgency and necessity.

Examples of expenses that might justify using your emergency fund include:

An urgent home repair
An unexpected medical expense
An essential car repair
Essential costs after an income loss

Whether an expense qualifies depends on your circumstances. For example, a broken appliance may need immediate replacement if it is essential to your household, while replacing a working appliance simply because you want a newer model is usually a planned purchase.

When deciding whether to use your fund, ask yourself: Is the expense important? Is it unexpected? Can it reasonably wait until you have saved for it separately? These questions can help you make a more considered decision.

PROTECT YOUR SAVINGS

Keep planned purchases separate.

When you see money sitting in an account, it can be tempting to use it for something you would like to buy. However, using your emergency fund for planned purchases reduces the money available if an unexpected expense occurs.

Can this expense wait until I save for it? ?

For example, if you want a new phone, a vacation, or new clothes, consider creating a separate savings goal for that purchase. You can then track the money you set aside without reducing your emergency reserve.

Some situations are less clear-cut. If your phone breaks and you need it for work or essential communication, replacing it may be necessary. Consider whether it can be repaired, whether you need a replacement immediately, and what affordable options are available.

The aim is not to follow a rigid rule regardless of the situation. It is to make a deliberate choice about when using your emergency savings is justified.

WHEN YOU NEED IT

Using your emergency fund does not mean you failed.

An emergency fund is meant to be used when an appropriate need arises. The goal is not to keep the balance untouched forever, but to have money available when you need it.

AFTER €600 Remaining savings

Imagine that you have saved €1,000 and need to spend €400 on an unexpected essential repair. After paying the expense, you have €600 remaining.

Your balance has decreased, but your savings helped cover the expense. Without that money, you might have needed to borrow, delay another payment, or use funds intended for something else.

After dealing with the emergency, review your finances and decide when you can begin rebuilding the fund. You can return to your previous target gradually; there is no need to replace the entire amount at once.

ADAPT TO YOUR CIRCUMSTANCES

Adjust your savings when life changes.

Your income and expenses will not necessarily stay the same every month. You might be able to save more during a good month and need to reduce your contribution when unexpected costs or other obligations arise.

Lower contribution: €10
Regular contribution: €30
Higher contribution: €100

These are examples, not recommended minimums or amounts everyone should be able to save. Choose a contribution based on what remains after essential expenses and important obligations.

If your income increases or a regular expense ends, you could consider putting some of the extra money toward your emergency fund. If your finances become tighter, you can reduce or temporarily pause contributions.

A realistic plan is more useful than a target that repeatedly forces you to choose between saving and paying for essentials.

YOUR NEXT STEPS

A simple four-step plan to get started.

You do not need a complicated financial system to begin. Use the following steps to turn the idea of an emergency fund into a practical plan.

1. Choose your first target. Pick an achievable amount based on your circumstances. You can increase the target as your savings grow.

2. Decide what you can contribute. Choose a weekly or monthly amount that does not prevent you from covering essential expenses.

3. Record each contribution. Keep track of deposits and withdrawals so you know your current balance. If you withdraw money for an emergency, update the balance rather than counting the original contributions as money still available.

4. Review your progress. Check your fund periodically and adjust your target or contributions when your income, expenses, or responsibilities change.

For example, if you set a target of €300 and have saved €180, you have €120 remaining to reach that milestone. Knowing the difference between your current balance and your target helps you see what progress you have made.
